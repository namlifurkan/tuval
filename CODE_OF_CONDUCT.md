# Code of conduct

This project is small and the people in it are here to build something. The rules are short
because the situation is simple.

## What is expected

Argue about the work. Disagreement is most of what a review is for, and a project that cannot
take "this is wrong, and here is why" produces worse software than one that can.

Say what you mean plainly, and take the same in return. Assume the person on the other side read
the code and thought about it before writing.

## What is not

- Insults, harassment, or personal attacks — including ones dressed as technical criticism
- Comments about somebody's identity rather than their argument: who they are, where they are
  from, what they believe, how they speak or write
- Unwanted attention after being asked to stop
- Publishing somebody's private information
- Deliberately wasting other people's time: bad-faith reports, reopening settled decisions to
  relitigate them, contributions written to make a point rather than to work

## Where it applies

Anywhere this project is: issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and any space
that represents it elsewhere.

## Reporting

Write to **product@tuval.dev**. Reports are read by the maintainer and nobody else, and are not
forwarded to the person reported. Say what happened and link to it if it is public.

What happens next depends on what happened: a private word, an edited or removed comment, or a
block from the repository. You will be told which. A report made in good faith never costs the
person who made it anything, whatever the outcome — and that includes a report that turns out to
be a misunderstanding.

The maintainer is subject to this document like everybody else. If the problem is the
maintainer, say so in the report; there is nobody else to escalate to yet, and pretending
otherwise would be the dishonest half.
